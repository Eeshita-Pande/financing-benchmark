# Accounting policy, operations and opening balances

This is the complete recognition policy for the exercise. No additional accounting-standard research or policy election is needed.

## Policy 1. Revenue and customer liabilities

Anchor fixed service revenue is earned evenly over each service month. Advance receipt is not revenue. Credits using that advance settle the month's charge at face value without changing the consideration for service. An unpaid or unapplied amount remains a customer obligation until validly applied or refunded.

Usage customer revenue is recognised in the service month at metered hours times the contractual rate, regardless of invoice issue or payment timing. The monthly receivable measure includes earned but not yet issued invoices; no separate contract-asset presentation is required here.

For the minimum customer, recognise the value of metered service as it is delivered. Unused minimum amounts allocated to make-up rights are deferred. Subsequent use of those rights releases the related deferred amount as part of revenue for that service; it is not revenue in addition to the value of service delivered. The deferred amount associated with an unused entitlement becomes revenue at its contractual expiry. Do not estimate early breakage. These rules also apply to the supplied opening credit vintages.

## Policy 2. Receivables and opening amounts

Receivables are gross collectible charges after invoice credit, reduced when cash is collected. Opening receivables arise from service and billing before the forecast. Their collection does not create forecast revenue. Collection delays do not change services provided or recognition under this policy.

The two opening make-up vintages total $50,000. Their issue precedes the forecast; their expiry months are in Opening. They are separate from the $150,000 opening minimum-customer receivable. Both balances are genuine and must be carried into the forecast. The opening statement is supplied, not reconstructed from invented historical usage.

## Policy 3. Profit and cash

Operating profit means EBIT. Show depreciation within operating profit and financing interest and agent fees below it. Principal, capex, sponsor contributions, advances and credit settlements are not operating expenses. There is no tax, FX, bad-debt provision or interest income.

## Policy 4. Equipment

New hardware is depreciated straight-line with zero residual value over the life in Terms, beginning in the anchor's first service month. Depreciation continues for the remaining economic life even if a customer contract has ended. Existing opening equipment has the stated net book value and remaining life and is depreciated evenly beginning in forecast month 1. No further capex, disposals or asset recoveries occur in the forecast.

## Operations 1. Operating expense and settlement

Source records supply hosting expense, payroll, power cost per metered hour and each customer's metered hours. Anchor hours are effective only during its contracted service, including renewal. Usage and minimum-customer hours are effective in the specified calendar month. Capacity is sufficient; there is no additional utilisation constraint.

Power expense is the current power rate applied to all effective metered hours. Hosting and power are expensed in the stated month and paid after their respective whole-month lags. Payroll is expensed and paid in the same month. The incident is expensed once in its stated month and paid after its own lag. The incident changes no other driver. Payment of opening supplier balances is additional to settlement of forecast expenses.

## Opening 1–4. Carry-in balances

Opening cash is unrestricted. The two customer receivables and two operating payables are paid in the months shown in Opening. For the two make-up vintages, that column instead identifies expiry. Initial loan principal and the anchor customer advance balance are zero. The agreed opening equity acquisition price equals the baseline opening cash plus receivables plus equipment less operating payables and the make-up liability. It is a fixed acquisition price for the return convention, not a new cash source or an automatic repricing formula when individual opening-balance sensitivities change.
